Privacy

On Your Right, the game. Last updated 14 November 2026.

The short version: there are no accounts, no ad networks and no tracking. The game only stores what it needs to keep your score and run the leaderboard. Here is everything.

On your device

The game remembers a few things on your phone or in your browser, and nowhere else: your best score, your total steps, the walkers and challenges you have unlocked, the walker you picked, the name you used on the leaderboard, and your sound settings. Delete the app (or clear the site's data in your browser) and it is all gone.

The online leaderboard

If you tap Post score, the game sends the name you typed (up to 12 letters and numbers), your score, your walker and how long the run lasted. These are stored in our database and shown to everyone on the Top 10. Please don't use your real full name or anything personal as your leaderboard name.

Names are filtered for rude words, and you can report a name with the little flag next to it. To have one of your own entries removed, email us (see below).

Anonymous play stats

When a run or a mini-game ends, the game sends a small anonymous note so we can balance the difficulty: how many steps you got, what hit you, how long it lasted, which walker you played, whether the screen was phone-shaped, and whether you won a mini-game. It has no name, no device ID and no account attached. We can't tell who you are from it.

What we don't do

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No advertising networks, no tracking across apps or websites, no analytics tools from other companies.
  • No access to your location, contacts, photos or microphone.
  • We never sell your data.

The Share button builds a score picture on your device and hands it to your phone's share menu. We never receive that picture.

The in-game "ads"

The ad breaks are jokes we made ourselves. One of them is for a real sponsor, DUDE London. If you tap its button twice it opens their website, which has its own privacy policy.

Who handles the data

The leaderboard and stats live in a database run by Supabase. The website is hosted by GitHub Pages, so, like any website, its servers can see your internet address when you open playcanalchaos.com. The apps carry the game inside them and only talk to the database when you view or post a score.

Children

On Your Right isn't aimed at children under 13, and it doesn't ask anyone for personal information.

Your choices

You can play without ever posting a score. You can ask us to remove a leaderboard entry, or ask what is stored about a name, by emailing us.
